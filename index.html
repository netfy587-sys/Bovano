Why maps never look quite right

Earth is a sphere, and a flat map must stretch or squeeze it somewhere. The result is called a projection, and every projection distorts something: shape, area, distance or direction.

The Mercator map, published by Gerardus Mercator in 1569, keeps directions true, which helped sailors. However, it makes regions near the poles look much larger than they are. On that map Greenland appears about as big as Africa, yet Africa is roughly fourteen times larger.

Article · 5 min

How time zones came to be

The Earth turns once about every 24 hours, so noon arrives at different moments in different places. In 1884, delegates at the International Meridian Conference in Washington, D.C. chose the meridian passing through Greenwich, England as the prime meridian.

That choice became the starting point for measuring longitude and for the system of world time zones used today.

Article · 5 min

The science behind the northern lights

The aurora forms when charged particles from the Sun are guided by Earth's magnetic field toward the polar regions. There they collide with gases in the upper atmosphere and release light. Oxygen can produce green and red glows, while nitrogen can add blue and purple tones.

The best viewing is usually on dark, clear winter nights in places such as northern Norway, Iceland, Finland, Canada and Alaska.

Article · 5 min

Coral reefs: small area, large impact

The Great Barrier Reef off the coast of Queensland, Australia, stretches for about 2,300 kilometres. Reefs like it provide shelter for fish, protect coastlines from waves and support millions of people through fishing and tourism.

Reefs are sensitive to rising water temperatures, which is why scientists monitor them closely.

Article · 5 min

Safe & Secure: travel basics

Good habits make travel calmer. Share your plans with someone you trust, keep copies of important documents, and carry only the cash and cards you need.

Online, use strong and different passwords, turn on two-step verification, and avoid logging in to banking or email accounts on public Wi-Fi unless the connection is protected.

Geography terms, explained

Words you will meet often, in simple language.

Latitude
How far a place is north or south of the equator, measured in degrees.
Longitude
How far a place is east or west of the prime meridian, measured in degrees.
Prime meridian
The line of 0° longitude, which passes through Greenwich, England.
Projection
A method of showing the round Earth on a flat map.
Time zone
A region that keeps the same standard time.
Ecosystem
A community of living things and the environment they share.
